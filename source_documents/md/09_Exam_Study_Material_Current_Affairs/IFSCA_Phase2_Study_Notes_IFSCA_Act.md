**IFSCA Act & Related Concepts**

* IFSCA Act

**1. Purpose & Scope**

* It applies specifically to financial services, financial products and financial institutions operating in an international financial services centre [IFSC] set up under the Special Economic Zones Act, 2005 (SEZ Act)
* **The Act thereby creates a regulatory regime for IFSCs in India—enabling entities to carry out cross-border financial operations under a unified regulator rather than multiple domestic regulators.**

**2. Key Definitions & Application**

* **“Appropriate regulator”** means any financial sector regulator listed in the **First Schedule of the Act** (for example, Reserve Bank of India (RBI), Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI), Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI), etc.).

Under the *IFSC Act, 2019*, the “appropriate regulators” originally were:
* **RBI** – banking,
* **SEBI** – capital markets,
* **IRDAI** – insurance,
* **PFRDA** – pensions.

After the IFSCA was established in 2020, it became the **single unified regulator** for all financial products, services, and institutions operating within **India’s International Financial Services Centres (e.g., GIFT City)**—replacing the earlier multiple regulators.

* **“Financial institution”** is defined in Section 3(c) as a unit set up in an IFSC engaged in rendering financial services in respect of any financial product.
* **“Financial product”** is defined in Section 3(d) to include **securities, contracts of insurance, deposits, credit arrangements, foreign currency contracts** (other than immediate currency exchanges) among others.

**3. Institutional Structure (Authority)**

* The Act establishes the International Financial Services Centres Authority (IFSCA) (Section 4).
* Composition:
  * A Chairperson.
  * Members nominated by RBI, SEBI, IRDAI, and PFRDA.
  * **Two members from the Ministry of Finance.**

**3. International Financial Services Centre (IFSC) – Concept and Indian Context**

**Concept**
* An IFSC is a jurisdiction that provides financial services to non-resident and international clients, often in foreign currency, and operates under a regulatory, fiscal and operational regime tailored for global financial transactions.
* In India's context, an *IFSC is created under* ***Section 18 of the SEZ Act,*** *2005, and then the IFSCV Act applies to such centres.*

* The objective is to *“on-shore the offshore”* — bring back international financial services which might otherwise occur offshore, into India under Indian jurisdiction but global standards.
* Key features of Indian IFSCs (as typified by GIFT IFSC) include:
  * Full convertibility of foreign currencies for eligible transactions.
  * Global-benchmark regulation and unified regulator (IFSCA).
  * Tax/other incentives, ease of doing business, proximity to India's domestic market.
  * Infrastructure support: physical (offices, towers, smart-city features) and digital (single-window approvals, global connectivity).

**The International Financial Services Centres Authority Act, 2019 (IFSCA Act)**

**Objective, Scope, and Key Provisions**

The **International Financial Services Centres Authority Act, 2019** was enacted by Parliament in **December 2019** to establish a **unified regulator** for India’s International Financial Services Centres (IFSCs).

*[Page 14 of 104]*

---

* **Objective:** To develop and regulate a **world-class international financial centre** in India through a **single-window regulatory framework** for cross-border financial services.
* ==**Enforcement:** Came into effect in **phases (April & September 2020)**; administered by the **Ministry of Finance**==.

**1. Establishment of IFSCA (Section 4)**
* Constitutes a corporate body titled **International Financial Services Centres Authority (IFSCA)**.
* Enjoys **perpetual succession** and a **common seal**.
* **Head Office:** Located in **GIFT City, Gandhinagar (Gujarat)**, with powers to establish other offices.

**2. Unified Regulatory Framework**
* Transfers the powers of **RBI, SEBI, IRDAI, and PFRDA** (for IFSC activities) to IFSCA.

**4. Legal and Currency Provisions**
* ==IFSC financial transactions are to be conducted in **foreign currency** (as notified by IFSCA in consultation with the Central Government)==.

**5. Legal Framework and Jurisdiction**
* ==IFSCs must be established **within SEZs** under the **SEZ Act, 2005**; only **one IFSC per SEZ** is permitted==.
* IFSCA exercises powers of the financial regulators for IFSC-related activities (as per the **First Schedule**).

**The International Financial Services Centres Authority (IFSCA)**
* **2. Regulatory Powers & Functions**
==**Statutory mandate:** To **regulate and develop** financial products, services, and institutions within IFSCs.==

**Key Powers:**
* **Licensing & Regulation:** Issue authorizations for banks, exchanges, insurers, and funds.
* **Section 12(2):** Regulate any financial service or product within the IFSC.
* **Section 13:** Transfers **"all powers" of RBI, SEBI, IRDAI, and PFRDA** to IFSCA for IFSC-related functions.

**3. Developmental Role**
* ==Promotes growth of the IFSC ecosystem through **single-window approvals, informal guidance schemes**, and **global partnerships**==.

4. **Innovation & FinTech Focus**
* **Regulatory Sandbox (2020):** FinTechs test new ideas under controlled conditions.
* **Innovation Sandbox:** Enables market institutions to experiment with data-driven solutions.
* Objective: Create a **global FinTech hub** at GIFT IFSC.

5. **Supervisory & Adjudicatory Powers**
* Can **impose penalties, cancel licenses, or issue enforcement actions.**

6. **Significance**

3. **Basics of Derivatives: Forwards, Futures, Swaps**

**A. Concept and Rationale**
* **Definition:** A derivative is a financial contract whose value is derived from an underlying asset such as a commodity, currency, interest rate, equity, or index.
* **Core Functions:**
  * **Hedging:** Reduces risk by locking in future prices or rates.
  * **Speculation:** Enables traders to profit from expected price movements.
  * **Arbitrage:** Exploits price differences across markets for low-risk profit.
* **Participants:** Hedgers (producers, corporates), Speculators (traders), and Arbitrageurs (market-neutral profit seekers).

**B. Forwards, Futures, and Swaps - Key Features**
1. **Forward Contracts**
   * **Meaning:** Private, **customized OTC agreements** to buy or sell an asset at a specific future date and price.
   * **Features:**
     * Custom-tailored terms (quantity, price, maturity).
     * Higher counterparty risk (no clearinghouse).
     * Settlement on maturity (delivery or cash).

2. **Futures Contracts**
   * **Meaning:** Standardized exchange-traded contracts to buy/sell an asset at a future date and price.

*[Page 45 of 104]*

---

* **Features:**
    * Traded on exchanges (MCX, NSE, BSE).
    * **Central clearing reduces default risk.**
    * Daily **mark-to-market** (MTM) settlement via margins.

### 3. Swap Contracts

* Meaning: **OTC contracts** where two parties exchange future cash flows based on predetermined terms.
* **Types:**
    * **Interest Rate Swap (IRS):** Exchange fixed vs. floating interest payments.
    * **Currency Swap:** Exchange cash flows in different currencies.
    * **Commodity Swap:** Exchange payments linked to commodity prices.
* **Use:** Long-term exposure management (interest rate or forex risk).

**3. Core Concepts: Risk, Uncertainty, Pooling, Diversification, Indemnity, Insurable Interest**

**a. Risk**

* **Definition:** Probability of loss due to uncertain future events.
* **Types:**
  * *Pure Risk:* Only loss (e.g., accident, death) — insurable.
  * *Speculative Risk:* Gain or loss (e.g., investment) — not insurable.

**b. Uncertainty**

* **Meaning:** Lack of complete knowledge about outcomes.
* **Insurance** transforms **uncertainty into measurable risk** via actuarial methods and probability.

**c. Risk Pooling**

* Collective contribution of many insured persons into a **common fund** to compensate the few who suffer losses.
* Reduces individual financial burden; stabilizes loss exposure through ***law of large numbers*.**

**d. Diversification**

* Spreading risk across different types of policies, geographies, or assets to reduce overall exposure.
* *Example:* Insurers invest premiums in varied asset classes (equity, bonds, reinsurance).

**e. Indemnity**

* Central to property and liability insurance.

*[Page 55 of 104]*

---

* Restores insured to pre-loss financial position — **no profit motive**.
* *Example*: Rs. 5 lakh fire damage $\rightarrow$ insurer pays Rs. 5 lakh (not more).

**f. Insurable Interest**

* Ensures insurance is not a gambling contract.
* Exists when loss or damage would cause *financial detriment* to insured.
* Must exist **at the time of policy (life)** or **both at inception and loss (property)**.

**2. Valuation and Solvency Requirements in Insurance**

Insurance companies must maintain adequate financial strength to honour policyholder claims, which is ensured through **asset and liability valuations** and **solvency regulations**.

**A. Valuation Principles**

**Objective:** To assess the insurer's true financial position — determining policyholder liabilities, reserves, and capital adequacy.

* **Valuation of Liabilities:**
  * Based on **Actuarial Valuation (Life Insurance):** present value of future obligations minus future premiums.
  * For General Insurance: estimation of *Outstanding Claims Reserve (OCR), Unexpired Risk Reserve (URR), and IBNR (Incurred But Not Reported)* reserves.
* **Valuation of Assets:**

**1. Reinsurance: Concept and Importance**

**A. Meaning**

* **Reinsurance** is the process by which an insurance company (the **ceding insurer**) transfers part of its risk to another insurance company (the **reinsurer**) in exchange for a premium.

*[Page 74 of 104]*

---

* It is essentially **“insurance of insurance”** — a second layer of protection that stabilizes the insurer’s financial performance and solvency.

## B. Objectives of Reinsurance

1. **Risk Diversification:** Spread risk across insurers and countries to avoid concentration.
2. **Stabilization of Results:** Reduce profit/loss volatility due to catastrophic events.
3. **Increase Underwriting Capacity:** Enables insurers to issue larger policies than their capital would otherwise allow.
4. **Catastrophic Protection:** Provides cover against low-frequency, high-severity events (earthquakes, cyclones).
5. **Capital Relief and Solvency Management:** Strengthens solvency ratio by transferring liabilities.
6. **Knowledge Transfer:** Global reinsurers assist Indian insurers with actuarial expertise and risk modeling.

Underwriting in insurance is the process where an insurance company evaluates the risk of an applicant to decide whether to accept the risk, determine the policy terms (like coverage limits and exclusions), and set the premium

## C. Types of Reinsurance

| Basis | Type | Description | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **By agreement** | *Facultative Reinsurance* | Individual policy or risk ceded specifically; suited for large or unusual risks. | A nuclear plant or satellite launch. |
| | *Treaty Reinsurance* | Automatic cession for a class of business under a standing agreement. | All motor or fire policies up to a limit. |
| **By sharing of loss** | *Proportional* | Premium and losses shared in fixed ratio (quota share, surplus). | 40% of every motor policy ceded. |
| | *Non-Proportional* | Reinsurer pays only when losses exceed a threshold (excess of loss, catastrophe). | Reinsurer pays if fire loss > ₹10 crore. |

**3. Obligatory Cessions**

**A. Meaning**

* An **obligatory cession** is the **mandatory portion of every general insurance policy** that Indian insurers must reinsure with the national reinsurer (GIC Re).

**B. Legal Framework**

* Governed by **Section 101A of the Insurance Act, 1938** and **IRDAI (Reinsurance) Regulations, 2018**.
* The **IRDAI** specifies annually:
  * The *percentage of risk* to be compulsorily ceded.
  * The *classes of business* covered.
  * The *terms and conditions* for such cessions.

**C. Current Practice (as per IRDAI Order for FY2024-25):**

* **Obligatory cession to GIC Re: 4%** of the sum insured on each general insurance policy (except specific exclusions like reinsurance, aviation hull, and oil & energy risks).
* **Retention by Indian insurers:** Balance 96% can be retained or placed with other reinsurers (domestic or foreign).

**Historical Trend:**

* Earlier (2000s): Obligatory cession up to 20%.
* Gradual reduction $\rightarrow$ 5% $\rightarrow$ 4% (reflecting market liberalisation and foreign participation).

**D. Significance of Obligatory Cession**

1. **Strengthens Domestic Reinsurance Pool:** Keeps part of premium within the country.
2. **Develops Local Expertise:** GIC Re gains underwriting experience across all classes.
3. **Improves National Retention Ratio:** Reduces outflow of foreign exchange.
4. **Supports Financial Stability:** Ensures capacity during catastrophic losses.

**4. Retention of Risk within the Country**

**A. Concept**

* **"Retention"** refers to the portion of risk that insurers **keep for themselves**, rather than transferring to reinsurers.
* **Retention within India** means ensuring that a substantial part of India's reinsurance premium and capacity remains in Indian hands (GIC Re and local insurers).

**B. IRDAI's Retention Policy Objectives**

1. **Conserve Domestic Capital:** Limit foreign reinsurance outflow.
2. **Build National Capacity:** Encourage local reinsurers and risk management capability.
3. **Strategic Autonomy:** Protect India's ability to cover large domestic risks (e.g., infrastructure, agriculture, defence).
4. **Gradual Market Liberalization:** Balance between competition (foreign reinsurers) and domestic strength (GIC Re).

---

**Order of Preference Rule (2018):**
IRDAI set a clear hierarchy for *who gets the first opportunity* to take Indian insurers' reinsurance business:

1. **Indian Reinsurers (like GIC Re):**
   Indian companies must first offer reinsurance to local players — mainly the state-owned **General Insurance Corporation of India (GIC Re)**.
2. **Foreign Reinsurer Branches (FRBs) in India:**
   If GIC Re cannot absorb all the risk, insurers can then offer it to foreign reinsurance companies that have *branches* registered and operating within India.
3. **Cross-Border Reinsurers (CBRs):**
   Only when domestic and local foreign branches don't have enough capacity, insurers can approach *overseas reinsurers* (those without a presence in India).

2. Principles of Insurance

| Principle | Meaning | Key Implication |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Utmost Good Faith (*Uberrimae Fidei*)** | Both insurer and insured must disclose all material facts truthfully. | Non-disclosure can void policy. |
| **Insurable Interest** | The insured must suffer loss if the insured event occurs. | Prevents wagering contracts. |
| **Indemnity** | Compensation equals actual loss (no profit). | Ensures financial restoration, not gain. |
| **Contribution** | Multiple insurers covering the same risk share claims proportionally. | Prevents over-indemnification. |
| **Subrogation** | After paying a claim, insurer acquires insured's right to recover from third parties. | Prevents double recovery. |
| **Risk Pooling & Law of Large Numbers** | Premiums from many policyholders fund claims for the few who suffer losses. | Reduces uncertainty through diversification. |

3. Risk and Uncertainty

* Risk: Quantifiable — measurable probability (e.g., mortality, accident rates).
* Uncertainty: Unquantifiable — unknown probability (e.g., war, pandemic).
* Insurance covers only fortuitous, measurable risks.

In insurance, "fortuitous" means a loss is accidental, unforeseen, and occurs by chance. This is a fundamental principle, as insurance contracts are intended to cover events that are not certain to happen and are outside the reasonable control of the insured and the insurer. Examples of fortuitous losses include damage from an unexpected fire, theft, or natural disaster

---

Law of Large Numbers: Larger the pool $\rightarrow$ closer actual losses to expected losses
$\rightarrow$ more stable premiums.
Diversification: Spread risk across sectors, geographies, and products to limit
correlated losses.
