[A] Majestic

[B] Economic

[C] External

[D] Mesmerizing

[E] Countering

# Explanation

Q.1) Explanation:

* All the events which can be measured in **terms of money** are classified as **economic events** and are recorded in the financial statements. **Hence, Option B is the correct answer.**
* It includes all the events like the *purchase of materials, sale of goods, acquisition of machinery, etc.*

**Additional Information:**

  * Sale of merchandise to the customers.

  * Purchase of materials from suppliers.
  * Payment of monthly rent to the landlord, etc.
* An **internal event** is an economic event that occurs entirely between the **internal wings** of an enterprise,
  **Examples:**
  * Supply of raw materials or components by the inventory department to the manufacturing department.
  * Payment of wages to the employees, etc.

[logo: EduTap] Explanation

# Business Entity Concept

* The **Business Entity Concept** assumes that a business has a **distinct and separate entity from its owners**.
* It means that for the purposes of accounting, the business and its owners are to be treated as two separate entities.
* If an owner invests money in the business, it will be treated as a **liability (capital)** for the business. **Hence, Option B is the correct answer.**
* However, if the owner takes out some money from the business for personal use, it will be considered **drawings**.
* The **accounting records** are made in the books of accounts from the point of view of the business unit and not that of the owner.
* **Example:** The amount of Rs. 1,00,000 invested in ABC Ltd. by **its owner will be considered a liability to the business.**

![illustration of a business building and an owner separated by arrows with the text 'Separate' above and 'Business' and 'Owner' below]

## Additional Information:

* **Going Concern Concept** assumes that a business firm would continue to carry out its operations indefinitely, i.e. for a fairly long period of time, and would not be liquidated in the foreseeable future. **Hence, Option A is incorrect.**
* The **Matching Concept** states that expenses incurred in an accounting period should be matched with revenues during that period. It follows from this that the revenue and expenses incurred to earn these revenues must belong to the same accounting period. **Hence, Option C is incorrect.**
* The **Revenue Recognition Concept** requires that the revenue for a business transaction should be included in the accounting records only when it is realized. **Hence, Option D is incorrect.**

Mohan purchased goods for Rs 15,00,000 and sold 4/5th of the goods for Rs 18,00,000 and met expenses amounting Rs 2,50,000 during the year, 2015. He counted the net profit as Rs 3,50,000. Which of the accounting concept was followed by him?  
[A] Objectivity Concept  
[B] Matching Concept  
[C] Conservatism Concept  
[D] Cost Concept  
[E] None of the above  

Answer: B

*[Page 12 of 39]*

[logo: EduTap] Explanation

Matching Concept
- As per **Matching Concept**, While calculating the net profit, expenses of the particular duration should be deducted from the respective revenue.
  - Here, the revenue = 18,00,000 and Purchase for the year = 15,00,000 x 4/5 = 12,00,000.
  - Expenses for the year = 250000
  - Therefore, Net Profit = 18,00,000 - 12,00,000 - 2,50,000= 3,50,000. **Hence, Option B is the correct answer.**
- The matching concept states that an organization should **recognize its expenses in the same financial year if the expense is related to the revenue of that year.**
- This concept is based on the **accounting period concept.** An important objective of business is to determine profit periodically.
- It is necessary to match the '**revenues**' of the period with the '**expenses**' of that period to determine the correct profit (or loss) for the accounting period.
- Profit earned by the business during a period can be correctly measured only when the revenue earned during the period is matched with the expenditure incurred to earn that revenue.
- It is not relevant when the payment was made or received.
- Therefore, as per this concept, adjustments are made for all outstanding expenses, prepaid expenses, accrued income, unearned income, etc.
- In brief, according to this concept, the **expenses for an accounting period are matched against related revenues**, rather than cash received and cash paid.
- This concept should be followed while preparing financial statements to have a true and fair view of the profitability and financial position of a business firm.
- **For example,** A company acquires production equipment for Rs 1,00,000 that has a projected useful life of 10 years. It should charge the cost of the equipment to depreciation expense at the rate of Rs 10,000 per year for ten years so that the expense is recognized over the entirety of its useful life.

Additional Information:
- The **Objectivity Concept** requires that accounting transactions should be recorded in an objective manner, free from the bias of accountants and others. **Hence, Option A is incorrect.**

Income and Expenditure Account is

(b) Personal Account
(c) Nominal Account
(d) Capital Account
(e) None of the above

Answer: C

[globe icon] www.edutap.in ✉ hello@edutap.co.in 📞 +91 81462-07241

# Explanation

The **Income and Expenditure Account** is similar in nature to a **Profit and Loss Account** and is used by **non-profit organizations** (like clubs, trusts, societies) to determine the surplus or deficit during a specific accounting period.  
Here's why it's classified as a **Nominal Account**:  
• **Nominal accounts** relate to **expenses, losses, incomes, and gains.**  
• The **Income and Expenditure Account** records **all revenues and expenses** of the organization for the year.  
• It shows whether the organization has made a **surplus (income > expenditure)** or a **deficit (expenditure > income).**  
This account **does not carry forward balances** to the next year—its balance is transferred to the **Capital Fund** (or General Fund), which is a part of the balance sheet.

# Explanation

When goods previously purchased are returned to the supplier, the business records this in a **Purchase Returns Journal** (also called Return Outwards Journal). This journal accumulates all such returns over a period (typically a month), and at the end of that period, a **periodic total** is posted to the **Purchase Returns Account** in the ledger.  
Here's a breakdown:  
• **Purchase Account (Option i):** This is used to record purchases of goods, not returns.  
• **Profit and Loss Account (Option ii):** This is used at the end of an accounting period to determine net profit or loss—not for direct posting of returns.  
• **Furniture Account (Option iv):** Only used when furniture is purchased or returned—not relevant to regular purchase returns unless it's specifically furniture being returned, which is uncommon in journal totals.  
Hence, **Option (iii)** is the most appropriate and standard accounting treatment.

Q.14

The periodic total of sales return journal is posted to :
(i) Sales account
(ii) Goods account
(iii) Purchases return account
(iv) Sales return account
(v) None of the above

Answer: (iv)

*[Page 31 of 39]*

---

[logo: EduTap]

# Explanation

The **Sales Return Journal** (also known as the **Return Inwards Journal**) is used to record all goods returned by customers. These are previously sold items now being returned, usually due to defects, damage, or dissatisfaction.

At the end of a period (weekly, monthly, etc.), the **total of the Sales Return Journal is posted to the debit side of the Sales Return Account** in the general ledger. This reflects a reduction in total sales revenue.

Let's analyze the other options:

• **(i) Sales account:** This records only forward sales, not returns.

• **(ii) Goods account:** Not used in this context; it's not a standard ledger account in this case.

• **(iii) Purchases return account:** This is for goods returned to suppliers (outward returns), not customer returns.

Therefore, the correct and standard accounting treatment is to post the periodic total of the **Sales Return Journal to the Sales Return Account — Option (iv)**.
