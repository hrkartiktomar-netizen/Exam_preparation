## IFSCA Grade A (IT Stream) – Exam Structure

**Selection Process:**

Phase I $\to$ Phase II $\to$ Coding Test $\to$ Interview

### Phase I – Online Screening Examination

| Paper | Marks | Duration |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| Paper 1 (Reasoning, Quantitative Aptitude, English, General Awareness) | 100 | 60 min |
| Paper 2 (IT Specific – Objective) | 100 | 60 min |

Negative marking: 1/4th per incorrect answer. Marks are used only for shortlisting to Phase II.

### Phase II – Online Main Examination

| Paper | Marks | Type |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| Paper 1 (Essay, Precis, Comprehension) | 100 | Descriptive |
| Paper 2 (Advanced IT – Objective) | 100 | Objective |

Weightage in Final Merit:
* Paper $1 - 1/3$
* Paper $2 - 2/3$

Negative marking applicable in Paper 2 only.

### Coding Test (IT Stream Only)

The Coding Test is **qualifying in nature**. Failure to qualify results in immediate elimination from the selection process, irrespective of Phase II performance.

In our cycle, out of 19 candidates shortlisted for interview, only 12 were finally interviewed. The remaining candidates were disqualified at the Coding Test stage.

### Interview

Final Merit Composition:
* Phase II – 85%
* Interview – 15%

## 2 Coding Questions Strategy

The coding syllabus was largely aligned with SEBI IT Phase 2 (Paper 2). The questions were implementation-based and focused on core data structures and fundamental algorithms rather than advanced competitive programming concepts.

The following types of questions were asked:

1. Write a program for Bubble Sort from scratch (without using inbuilt sort functions).
2. Merge two sorted arrays and return the final sorted array (consider only valid non-zero elements).
3. Construct a Binary Search Tree (BST) from a given array and print its inorder traversal.
4. Construct a BST from a given array and compute its height.
5. Check whether a given string `s` occurs in another string `t` (substring check).
6. Implement Fibonacci using Dynamic Programming.
7. Find the minimum number of coins required (DP approach, not greedy).

### Preparation Strategy

If you solve the Easy and Medium problems from Striver's DSA Sheet, it will be more than sufficient for this section.

### Priority Areas

* Binary Trees (BT)
* Binary Search Trees (BST)
* Basic Sorting
* String Manipulation
* 1D Dynamic Programming

Trees (both BT and BST) should receive special attention, as multiple questions were directly based on construction, traversal, and structural properties.

For Dynamic Programming, focus only on standard easy-level problems such as Fibonacci, coin change (DP version), and similar 1D array-based formulations.

Graph problems are not required, as they are not mentioned in the syllabus.

### Exam-Oriented Insight

The coding section tests:

* Conceptual clarity of data structures
* Clean implementation
* Understanding of time and space complexity
* Ability to write logic from scratch

The questions are straightforward if fundamentals are clear. Strong command over Trees and basic DP ensures high scoring in this section.

## 3 Interview Preparation

The interview preparation was structured around three major pillars:

1. Technical Depth (IT Domain)
2. Organizational Awareness (IFSCA-specific knowledge)
3. Personal and Role Justification

### 1. Technical Preparation (IT-Focused)

For the IT stream, the interview is heavily technical. The panel primarily evaluates:

* Depth of understanding in core IT subjects
* Practical exposure in current work profile
* Conceptual clarity in AI/ML and Cybersecurity

A significant portion of the discussion revolves around the candidate's current organization and ongoing work. Therefore, it is important to:

* Clearly explain your current role and responsibilities
* Justify architectural and technical decisions taken in your projects
* Demonstrate real-world problem-solving exposure

AI/ML-related questions are usually theoretical and application-oriented rather than mathematical. Cybersecurity questions often focus on attack vectors, mitigation strategies, and real-world implementation scenarios.

### 2. IFSCA-Specific Preparation

Strong institutional awareness is expected. Preparation should include:

* Overview and objectives of IFSCA
* Key provisions of the IFSCA Act, 2019
* Regulatory role in IFSC (GIFT City)
* Recent initiatives and achievements

Understanding the working of the SWIT portal (IFSCA portal) is important, including its purpose, stakeholders, and regulatory workflow.

Candidates should be able to articulate:

* How IFSCA differs from RBI, SEBI, and IRDAI
* Its unified regulatory framework within IFSC
* Its vision of making India a global financial hub

### 3. Personal Justification and Motivation

"Why IFSCA?" is a critical question and must be answered convincingly. The response should reflect:

* Alignment between technical background and regulatory technology requirements
* Interest in financial technology, innovation, and policy-driven impact
* Long-term commitment rather than short-term career movement

The panel expects maturity, clarity of intent, and awareness of the responsibility associated with a regulatory role. **BE HONEST !!!**
