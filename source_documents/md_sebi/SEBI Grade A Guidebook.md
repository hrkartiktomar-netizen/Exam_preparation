# 2. SEBI Grade A Officer Job 💼

Before preparing for the exam, every aspirant should clearly understand what the SEBI Grade A Officer job involves, the roles and responsibilities, departments they'll handle, salary, tax deductions, allowances, perks, training, postings, transfers, and promotion structure.

Knowing these details helps you stay motivated and plan your career journey with clarity.

## 2.1 Role/Responsibilities of SEBI Assistant Manager 👨‍💼

As a Grade A Officer, you play a key role in regulating and developing India's securities market, handling responsibilities across multiple functions such as:

* **Market Surveillance:** Monitoring market activities for irregularities, insider trading, and other fraudulent practices.

* **Regulatory Compliance:** Ensuring that market participants adhere to SEBI regulations and guidelines.

* **Investigations:** Conducting investigations into suspected violations of securities laws.

* **Policy Formulation:** Assisting in the development and implementation of new regulations and policies.

* **Investor Protection:** Protecting the interests of investors and ensuring a fair and transparent market.

* **Data Analysis:** Analyzing market data and trends to identify potential risks and opportunities.

* **Corporate Governance:** Promoting good corporate governance practices among listed companies.

* **International Coordination:** Collaborating with international regulatory bodies.

Important Note: The role and responsibilities of a SEBI Grade A officer depend on the department to which they are assigned. The departments are assigned as per SEBI requirements.

Please note that the exact in-hand salary may vary from person to person depending on location, tax regime, and the allowances actually availed.

# 2.4 Training at SEBI 👨‍💻

* After selection, you first attend a **6-week induction program** at the **National Institute of Securities Markets (NISM), Patalganga, Maharashtra**.

* The training covers **technical knowledge of the securities market** and **soft skill development** through behavioural sessions.

* After the induction, you begin **on-the-job training** and serve a **2-year probation period**.

* **Full salary is paid** during the entire training period.

* The **SEBI Head Office** is in **Mumbai**, with **Regional Offices** in **New Delhi, Chennai, Ahmedabad, and Kolkata**, etc., and **local offices** across other major cities.

# 2.5 Postings 👨‍💼

Here is the posting policy of a SEBI Grade A officer:

* Most SEBI Grade A officers are initially posted at SEBI's headquarters in Mumbai, Maharashtra.

* SEBI does have regional offices in several key locations across India, including Delhi, Chandigarh, Bangalore, Chennai, Kolkata, and others.

* If you wish to be posted in your hometown, you may request the same to SEBI after joining.

* There are no rural postings in SEBI at all.

* SEBI does not have its office abroad, so you do not get posted abroad.

* You may get an opportunity to go on deputation to various other domestic regulatory bodies, Government departments, foreign regulatory bodies, multilateral organizations and other organizations relevant to the financial market.

# 2.6 Transfers 👨‍💼

Here are some key points about the transfer policy of SEBI Grade A officers:

* Transfers typically occur after completing 5 years in a particular posting.

* Transfers can be to any SEBI office in India, including:
  * **Head Office:** Mumbai
  * **Regional Offices:** Northern Regional Office (NRO) - New Delhi, Southern Regional Office (SRO) - Chennai, Western Regional Office (WRO) - Ahmedabad, Eastern Regional Office (ERO) - Kolkata, etc.
  * **Local Offices:** Indore Local Office, Delhi, Chandigarh, Bangalore, Chennai, Kolkata, etc.

## 4.1 SEBI Grade A - Phase 1 Pattern 📉

The Phase 1 (Prelims) examination is **qualifying in nature** and consists of **two papers**. Marks obtained in this stage are used **only for shortlisting candidates for Phase 2** and are **not considered for the final merit list.**

## 4.1.1 Phase 1 Paper 1 📑

- Contains **multiple-choice questions** from **General Awareness** (including basic financial awareness), **English Language**, **Quantitative Aptitude**, and **Reasoning**.

- The paper carries **100 marks**, is to be completed in **60 minutes**, and consists of **80 questions.**

- Each section has **20 questions** for **25 marks**, contributing to the total of 100 marks.

- The **minimum cut-off** for Paper 1 is **30%** (there is no sectional cut-off).

- **Negative marking of 1/4th of the marks** assigned to each question is applicable.

# 4.1.3 Overall Cut-off Requirement

- Candidates must secure:

- 

  - The **individual cut-off in both Paper 1 (30%)** and **Paper 2 (40%)**, and

  - An **aggregate cut-off of 40%** across Phase 1.

- Only candidates meeting **all** the above criteria are shortlisted for **Phase 2**.

- For example: If a student scores **32 marks in Paper 1** and **38 marks in Paper 2**, their **total becomes 70 marks**.

  - Even though they have cleared the Paper 1 cut-off, they have not cleared the Paper 2 cut-off (**minimum required is 40 marks**) or the overall aggregate cut-off (**minimum required is 80 marks**).

# 4.2 SEBI Grade A 2025 - Phase 2 Pattern 🗂️

Candidates who qualify Phase 1 will be issued **fresh hall tickets** for Phase 2. Phase 2 consists of **two papers**, and marks obtained in this stage are used for **shortlisting candidates for the Interview (Phase 3).**

* Candidates applying for **multiple streams** will have **Paper 2** conducted in **different shifts**, and the exact timings will be mentioned in the hall ticket.
* **Negative marking of 1/4th of the marks** assigned to each question applies to the MCQ-based portion of Paper 2.
* **Paper 1**
  * Carries **30% minimum cut-off**, as prescribed in the official notification.
  * Contributes **one-third (1/3rd)** to the overall Phase 2 aggregate.
* **Paper 2**
  * Carries a **40% minimum cut-off**.
  * Includes **MCQs**, with negative marking applicable.
  * Contributes **two-thirds (2/3rd)** to the overall Phase 2 aggregate.
  * **All papers**, except English, are **bilingual (Hindi & English)**.
* **Candidates must secure:**
  * The **individual cut-off** in both Paper 1 (**30%**) and Paper 2 (**40%**), and
  * An **aggregate of 50%** across both papers.
* Based on the **Phase 2 aggregate**, candidates meeting all cut-off conditions will be **shortlisted for the Interview**.
* The number of candidates shortlisted will be **three times the number of vacancies**, arranged in order of merit.
* The final list of candidates shortlisted for the Interview will be published on the **SEBI website**.

## 4.3 Changes in Phase 1 and Phase 2 Pattern (Across Cycles) 🔲

To give clarity on how the number of questions has changed across the recent cycles:

• In **2022**, Phase 1 Paper 2 and Phase 2 Paper 2 both consisted of **50 questions each.**

• In **2024**, Phase 1 Paper 2 had **50 questions**, but Phase 2 Paper 2 was **increased to 100 questions.**

• The **2025 notification** follows the **same pattern as 2024**, with Phase 1 Paper 2 having 50 questions and Phase 2 Paper 2 having 100 questions.

## 4.4 SEBI Grade A Interview 👩‍💼

The SEBI Grade A examination consists of an Interview.

• Candidates are shortlisted for interview based on their Phase 2 marks only (sum of 2 papers)

• Weightage of marks obtained in Phase 2 is 85%, while marks obtained in interview is given a weightage of 15%.

• Candidates can opt for interview either in Hindi or English

The final selection of candidates is done by adding marks secured in Phase 2 and Interview.

# 6. Cut Off Marks 

Knowing the SEBI Grade A cut-off marks for each stage over the last few exams helps you understand the competition and set clear score targets for every phase.

## 6.1 Last 3 Year Cut Off Marks Trends 

- **Phase 1 (Prelims)** is qualifying in nature with fixed cut-offs:
  - **Paper 1:** Minimum 30% (30/100)
  - **Paper 2:** Minimum 40% (40/100)

- You need to **clear both papers separately** and secure an **overall 40% aggregate (80/200)** to move to Phase 2.

- **No sectional cut-off** applies in Phase 1.

- **Phase 2 (Mains)** has **2 papers of 100 marks each:**
  - **Paper 1:** 30% Cut off
  - **Paper 2:** 40% Cut off
  - Total Phase 2 score is calculated out of 100 using these weightages.

- **Final Selection:**
  - **Phase 2 contributes 85%** of the final score
  - **Interview contributes 15%**
  - Both Phase 2 and Interview scores are scaled to 100 based on their weightages.

## 8.2.1 Detailed Application Form (DAF): 📑

DAF is the most important tool for your interview because it tells the panel members about you and helps them judge you.

If you have prepared at least 100 questions on your DAF including family background, educational qualification, gap years if any, low % in academics, etc. really well, you will be very easily able to turn the interview in your favour.

Also, while filling your DAF, you should avoid some of these common mistakes -

* Always give professional references so that they can vouch for your competitiveness and intelligence.
* Following a random order for filling educational qualifications
* Not giving honest reasons for leaving the previous job. You need to be honest yet careful while filling this section, as in you may write that for better career prospects you left the job but saying that you left the job because your boss was pathetic can-do harm.

The interviewer of any organization expects you to know at least a bit about the organization.

To know about SEBI, the About section, Annual reports, Press releases containing recent developments in SEBI and Speeches on the SEBI website are more than sufficient.

The interviewer may not ask direct questions but you can always use the information collected over the time to frame your answers in the interview.

# 8.4 SEBI Grade A: Subject-Wise Strategy 📋

### 8.4.1 Management

* Core syllabus topics: Management principles, Leadership, HRD, Motivation, Communication.

* Also cover **(not listed in syllabus but asked earlier):** Recruitment & Selection; Training & Development; Career Planning; Performance Appraisals; Counselling.

* Memorise keywords and trivial facts (e.g., Maslow's five needs). Practice many MCQs.

### 8.4.2 Costing

* 4 main units:

  1. Overview of Cost & Management Accounting (cost types)
  2. Costing methods — job, batch, contract, process, unit costing; costing for service sector
  3. Cost control — marginal costing, standard costing, budgeting
  4. Lean systems & innovation (waste reduction, defect reduction techniques)

* High-priority: Cost sheet, marginal costing / CVP analysis, material & labour variances, process costing (abnormal loss, equivalent production).

* Practice numerical problems thoroughly (Phase 1 had some numericals; Phase 2 had more).

### 8.4.3 Finance

* Unit A: Roles & functions of regulatory bodies (RBI, SEBI, NABARD, PFRDA etc.)

* Financial markets (money, bond, equity, forex) and instruments (derivatives, futures, forwards, swaps); basics of mutual funds.

* Financial inclusion, alternate finance (crowdfunding, PPP).

* Taxation basics (GST), Fiscal Policy, FRBM, Finance Commission.

* Time value of money basics; simple numerical practice for bonds/derivatives where required. Prioritise Unit A & core topics.

### 8.4.4 Company Law (selected chapters only)

* Mandatory chapters to cover: Ch. 3 (Prospectus & Allotment), Ch. 4 (Share Capital & Debentures), Ch. 8 (Dividends), Ch. 10 (Audit & Auditors), Ch. 11 (Appointment of Auditors), Ch. 12 (Qualification/Appointment of Directors), Ch. 27 (NCLT & NCLAT).

* Focus on facts and numbers (limits, thresholds, who can remove/appoint whom, key section numbers).

* Paper tends to be fact-based — memorise key provisions, section numbers, and factual details.

**8.4.5 Economics (selected)**

* Prioritise Unit B content (relatively easier): Balance of payments, Forex, inflation, monetary & fiscal policy, non-bank financial institutions.
* From Unit A pick: Inflation, business cycles, demand & supply of money — basic conceptual clarity.

**8.4.6 Quantitative Aptitude & Reasoning (Paper 1 focus)**

* Quant: Simplification/Approximation, Number series, Quadratic equations, DI (ratio & percentage basics). Target ~10 marks. Practice ~100 Qs from selected topics.
* Reasoning: Focus on Coding-Decoding, Syllogism, Blood relations, Direction, Puzzles/Arrangement styles prevalent in recent tests. Target ~10 marks.

**8.4.7 General Awareness**

* Continuous daily practice: current affairs, finance sector news, static facts relevant to SEBI and regulatory functions.
* During the final phase increase to 2 hrs/day as revision.

**8.4.8 Accounts (selected)**

* Cover selected portions only (after Phase 1 if self-prepping). Focus on topics that have appeared previously. Practice end-of-chapter problems in recommended books.

**8.5 Revision strategy**

* **Daily recall:** after studying a topic, close notes and recall key points aloud or write them. This strengthens permanent memory.
* **Parallel revision:** revise daily, again after one week, and after one month (1-1-1 rule).
* **Weekend deep revision:** invest 5–6 hours on weekends for consolidation.
* Solve lots of MCQs as part of revision — MCQs act as both practice and revision.

**8.6 Mock test strategy**

* Do **full-length mocks only after 70–80% syllabus completion**.
* **5 full-length mocks** are suggested.
* Leave **1 day gap** between mocks for analysis (e.g., Mon mock $\rightarrow$ Wed mock).
* Give mocks as **one full exam day** (Paper 1 + Paper 2 back-to-back) to simulate actual conditions.
* Analyse each mock thoroughly before the next one; focus on weak topics revealed by analysis.
