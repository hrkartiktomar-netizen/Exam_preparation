3. Basics of Derivatives: Forwards, Futures, Swaps

A. Concept and Rationale
     Denition: A derivative is a fnancial contract whose value is derived
          rom an underlying asset such as a commodity, currency, interest rate,
          equity, or index.
     Core Functions:
               o Hedging: Reduces risk by locking in uture prices or rates.
               o Speculation: Enables traders to proft rom expected price
                    movements.
               o Arbitrage: Exploits price dierences across markets or low-risk
                    proft.
     Participants: Hedgers (producers, corporates), Speculators (traders), and
          Arbitrageurs (market-neutral proft seekers).

B. Forwards, Futures, and Swaps – Key Features
1. Forward Contracts

     Meaning: Private, customized OTC agreements to buy or sell an asset
          at a specifc uture date and price.

     Features:
               o Custom-tailored terms (quantity, price, maturity).
               o Higher counterparty risk (no clearinghouse).
               o Settlement on maturity (delivery or cash).

2. Futures Contracts
     Meaning: Standardized exchange-traded contracts to buy/sell an asset at
          a uture date and price.
     Features:
               o Traded on exchanges (MCX, NSE, BSE).
               o Central clearing reduces default risk.
               o Daily mark-to-market (MTM) settlement via margins.

3. Swap Contracts
     Meaning: OTC contracts where two parties exchange uture cash ows
          based on predetermined terms.
     Types:
               o Interest Rate Swap (IRS): Exchange fxed vs. oating interest
                    payments.
               o Currency Swap: Exchange cash ows in dierent currencies.
               o Commodity Swap: Exchange payments linked to commodity
                    prices.
     Use: Long-term exposure management (interest rate or orex risk).

3. Core Concepts: Risk, Uncertainty, Pooling, Diversication, Indemnity,
Insurable Interest
a. Risk

     Denition: Probability o loss due to uncertain uture events.
     Types:

               o Pure Risk: Only loss (e.g., accident, death) — insurable.

               o Speculative Risk: Gain or loss (e.g., investment) — not insurable.

b. Uncertainty
     Meaning: Lack o complete knowledge about outcomes.
     Insurance transforms uncertainty into measurable risk via actuarial
          methods and probability.

c. Risk Pooling
     Collective contribution o many insured persons into a common fund to
          compensate the ew who suer losses.
          Reduces individual fnancial burden; stabilizes loss exposure through law
          of large numbers.

d. Diversication
     Spreading risk across dierent types o policies, geographies, or assets to
          reduce overall exposure.
     Example: Insurers invest premiums in varied asset classes (equity, bonds,
          reinsurance).

e. Indemnity
     Central to property and liability insurance.
     Restores insured to pre-loss fnancial position — no prot motive.
     Example: Rs. 5 lakh fre damage → insurer pays Rs. 5 lakh (not more).
f. Insurable Interest
     Ensures insurance is not a gambling contract.
     Exists when loss or damage would cause nancial detriment to insured.
     Must exist at the time of policy (life) or both at inception and loss

          (property).

2. Valuation and Solvency Requirements in Insurance

Insurance companies must maintain adequate fnancial strength to honour
policyholder claims, which is ensured through asset and liability valuations
and solvency regulations.

A. Valuation Principles

Objective: To assess the insurer’s true fnancial position — determining
policyholder liabilities, reserves, and capital adequacy.

     Valuation of Liabilities:

               o Based on Actuarial Valuation (Lie Insurance): present value o
                    uture obligations minus uture premiums.

               o For General Insurance: estimation o Outstanding Claims Reserve
                    (OCR), Unexpired Risk Reserve (URR), and IBNR (Incurred But Not
                    Reported) reserves.

     Valuation of Assets:
               o Carried at market or amortised cost depending on IRDAI
                    (Investment) Regulations, 2016.

               o Only “approved investments” (Government securities, bonds, high-
                    rated equities) count toward solvency.

Actuarial Oversight:
Each insurer must appoint an Appointed Actuary under IRDAI (Appointed
Actuary) Regulations, 2022 — responsible or:

     Pricing and valuation.
     Solvency testing.
     Product certifcation and stress testing.
An actuary is a proessional who assesses and manages fnancial risks and
uncertainty using mathematics, statistics, and fnancial theory.

B. Solvency Requirements
Meaning:
Solvency margin = excess o assets over liabilities required to ensure the insurer
can meet obligations under adverse conditions.

     Regulatory Framework:
          Section 64VA, Insurance Act, 1938 and IRDAI (Assets, Liabilities and
          Solvency Margin of Insurers) Regulations, 2016.

     Minimum Solvency Margin (MSM):
               o Life Insurers: 150% o required solvency margin.
               o General Insurers: 150% o required solvency margin.
               o Reinsurers: 200%.

Purpose:
     Protect policyholders against insolvency.
     Maintain confdence in the insurance system.
     Enable international equivalence (aligned with Solvency II and IAIS
          standards).

Example (India):
     LIC (FY2024) Solvency Ratio: ~185%.
     Private lie insurers (average): ~205%.
     IRDAI may introduce Risk-Based Capital (RBC) norms — aligning
          solvency with insurer’s specifc risk profle.

C. Emerging Global & Indian Reforms
 Risk-Based Solvency (RBS) ramework being implemented in India rom
     2025 (pilot stage).

 Shit toward IFRS 17 (Insurance Contracts) or valuation and disclosure
     — ensures comparability across jurisdictions.

A. Meaning

     An obligatory cession is the mandatory portion of every general

B. Legal Framework
     Governed by Section 101A of the Insurance Act, 1938 and IRDAI
          (Reinsurance) Regulations, 2018.
     The IRDAI specifes annually:
               o The percentage of risk to be compulsorily ceded.
               o The classes of business covered.
               o The terms and conditions or such cessions.

C. Current Practice (as per IRDAI Order for FY2024–25):
     Obligatory cession to GIC Re: 4% o the sum insured on each general

     Retention by Indian insurers: Balance 96% can be retained or placed

Historical Trend:
     Earlier (2000s): Obligatory cession up to 20%.
     Gradual reduction → 5% → 4% (reecting market liberalisation and oreign
          participation).

D. Signicance of Obligatory Cession
     1. Strengthens Domestic Reinsurance Pool: Keeps part o premium
          within the country.
     2. Develops Local Expertise: GIC Re gains underwriting experience across
          all classes.
     3. Improves National Retention Ratio: Reduces outow o oreign
          exchange.
     4. Supports Financial Stability: Ensures capacity during catastrophic

4. Retention of Risk within the Country

A. Concept

     “Retention” reers to the portion o risk that insurers keep for
          themselves, rather than transerring to reinsurers.

     Retention within India means ensuring that a substantial part o India’s
          reinsurance premium and capacity remains in Indian hands (GIC Re and
          local insurers).

B. IRDAI’s Retention Policy Objectives

     1. Conserve Domestic Capital: Limit oreign reinsurance outow.

     2. Build National Capacity: Encourage local reinsurers and risk
          management capability.

     3. Strategic Autonomy: Protect India’s ability to cover large domestic risks
          (e.g., inrastructure, agriculture, deence).

     4. Gradual Market Liberalization: Balance between competition (oreign
          reinsurers) and domestic strength (GIC Re).

Order of Preference Rule (2018):
IRDAI set a clear hierarchy or who gets the rst opportunity to take Indian
insurers’ reinsurance business:

     1. Indian Reinsurers (like GIC Re):
          Indian companies must frst oer reinsurance to local players — mainly the
          state-owned General Insurance Corporation of India (GIC Re).

     2. Foreign Reinsurer Branches (FRBs) in India:
          I GIC Re cannot absorb all the risk, insurers can then oer it to oreign
          reinsurance companies that have branches registered and operating
          within India.

     3. Cross-Border Reinsurers (CBRs):
          Only when domestic and local oreign branches don’t have enough
          capacity, insurers can approach overseas reinsurers (those without a

3. Risk and Uncertainty

          Risk: Quantiable — measurable probability (e.g., mortality,
          accident rates).

          Uncertainty: Unquantiable — unknown probability (e.g., war,
          pandemic).

          Insurance covers only fortuitous, measurable risks.

In insurance, "ortuitous" means a loss is accidental, unoreseen, and occurs by
chance. This is a undamental principle, as insurance contracts are intended to
cover events that are not certain to happen and are outside the reasonable
control o the insured and the insurer. Examples o ortuitous losses include
damage rom an unexpected fre, thet, or natural disaster
Law o Large Numbers: Larger the pool → closer actual losses to expected losses
→ more stable premiums.
Diversifcation: Spread risk across sectors, geographies, and products to limit
correlated losses.

3. Dened-Benet (DB) vs. Dened-Contribution (DC) Pensions

Dened-Benet (DB) Schemes:

     Promise a fxed pension based on a ormula (usually a raction o fnal
          salary).

     Examples: Pre-2004 government pensions, armed orces, railways, EPS-
          95.
     EPS formula: (Average last 60 months’ salary × service years) / 70 →
          capped ~50% replacement; minimum ₹1,000/month.

     Financed by the employer or the state; limited portability via “Scheme
          Certifcates.”

Dened-Contribution (DC) Schemes:
     Payout depends on contributions and investment returns.
     Examples: EPF, NPS, APY, PM-SYM, Superannuation Funds.
     EPF/EPS: Employee contributes 12% o basic pay; employer contributes
          12% (8.33% to EPS, 3.67% to EPF).
     NPS: Both employee and employer contribute fxed percentages; corpus
          invested in market instruments; at retirement, 60% lump-sum + 40%
          annuity.
     APY (2015): Fixed contribution → guaranteed pension
          ₹1,000–₹5,000/month at 60.
     PM-SYM (2019): ₹3,000/month pension or inormal workers.
     Corporate Superannuation Funds: Employer-unded (up to 15% o
          pay), paying partly lump-sum and partly annuity.
     Trend: Shit rom DB (state-backed) to DC (market-linked) to widen
          coverage.

4. National Pension System (NPS)
Nature and Eligibility

     Regulated by PFRDA.
     Open to all Indian citizens (including NRIs/OCIs) aged 18–70 years.
     Two accounts: Tier I (retirement) and Tier II (voluntary).
     Tax Benets:

               o Up to 10% o salary (basic + DA) deductible under Sec. 80CCD(1).
               o Additional ₹50,000 under Sec. 80CCD(1B).
Investment and Management
     Funded through Central Recordkeeping Agency (CRA) under PFRDA.
     Managed by 9 Pension Fund Managers (PFMs): SBI, LIC, UTI, Kotak,
          Axis, ICICI, Tata, Aditya Birla, HDFC.
     Subscribers may choose:
               o Active Choice: Allocate up to 75% in equities.
               o Auto Choice: Equity share reduces with age.
     Regulated to avoid speculative investments.
Withdrawal and Exit

     At 60: 60% tax-ree lump sum; 40% annuitized.
     Small corpus (≤ ₹2.5 lakh): ull withdrawal allowed.
     Early exit: 20% lump-sum allowed (≥ 10 yrs service), remainder

          annuitized.
     Partial withdrawals (up to 25%) allowed ater 10 years or specifc

          purposes.
     Upon death: ull payout to nominee.

5. Atal Pension Yojana (APY)
Purpose: Guaranteed pension or unorganized workers (18–40 years old) who
are non-taxpayers.
Structure:

     Subscribers select pension (₹1,000–₹5,000 at 60).
     Administered by PFRDA using NPS architecture.
Performance (as of April 2025):
     Subscribers: ~7.65 crore
     ~48% women participants.
Features:
     Lietime guaranteed pension with amily beneft.
     Automatic debit through bank/post oce channels.
6. Annuity Plans
Denition: Contracts (mainly with insurance companies) converting a lump-sum
into periodic lielong payments.
Types:
     1. Immediate Annuity: Payments start immediately ater paying lump-sum.
     2. Deferred Annuity: Payments begin ater a fxed deerment period (e.g.,

     3. Fixed/Guaranteed Annuity: Constant payout; stable but limited upside.
     4. Variable/Indexed Annuity: Payout linked to market returns or ination

Providers: LIC, HDFC Lie, SBI Lie, ICICI Pru, etc.

 Standardised product eatures and disclosure requirements.

     Function: Ensures steady lifelong income, complementing lump-sum
