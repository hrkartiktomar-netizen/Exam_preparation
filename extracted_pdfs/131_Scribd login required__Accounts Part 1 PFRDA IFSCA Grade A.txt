  Q.11

Income and Expenditure Account is
(a) Real Account
(b) Personal Account
(c) Nominal Account
(d) Capital Account
(e) None of the above

          Answer: C
                                             Explanation

The Income and Expenditure Account is similar in nature to a Profit and Loss Account and is used
by non-profit organizations (like clubs, trusts, societies) to determine the surplus or deficit during a
specific accounting period.
Here's why it's classified as a Nominal Account:
•Nominal accounts relate to expenses, losses, incomes, and gains.
•The Income and Expenditure Account records all revenues and expenses of the organization for
the year.
•It shows whether the organization has made a surplus (income > expenditure) or a deficit
(expenditure > income).
This account does not carry forward balances to the next year—its balance is transferred to the
Capital Fund (or General Fund), which is a part of the balance sheet.

  Q.13

The periodic total of purchases return journal is posted to :
(i) Purchase account
(ii) Profit and loss account
(iii) Purchase returns account
(iv) Furniture account
(v) None of the above

          Answer: iii
Explanation
                                           Explanation

When goods previously purchased are returned to the supplier, the business records this in a
Purchase Returns Journal (also called Return Outwards Journal). This journal accumulates all such
returns over a period (typically a month), and at the end of that period, a periodic total is posted to
the Purchase Returns Account in the ledger.
Here’s a breakdown:
•Purchase Account (Option i): This is used to record purchases of goods, not returns.
•Profit and Loss Account (Option ii): This is used at the end of an accounting period to determine
net profit or loss—not for direct posting of returns.
•Furniture Account (Option iv): Only used when furniture is purchased or returned—not relevant to
regular purchase returns unless it’s specifically furniture being returned, which is uncommon in journal
totals.
Hence, Option (iii) is the most appropriate and standard accounting treatment.
  Q.14

The periodic total of sales return journal is posted to :
(i) Sales account
(ii) Goods account
(iii) Purchases return account
(iv) Sales return account
(v) None of the above

         Answer: (iv)
                                           Explanation

The Sales Return Journal (also known as the Return Inwards Journal) is used to record all goods
returned by customers. These are previously sold items now being returned, usually due to defects,
damage, or dissatisfaction.
At the end of a period (weekly, monthly, etc.), the total of the Sales Return Journal is posted to the debit
side of the Sales Return Account in the general ledger. This reflects a reduction in total sales revenue.
Let’s analyze the other options:
•(i) Sales account: This records only forward sales, not returns.
•(ii) Goods account: Not used in this context; it's not a standard ledger account in this case.
•(iii) Purchases return account: This is for goods returned to suppliers (outward returns), not customer
returns.
Therefore, the correct and standard accounting treatment is to post the periodic total of the Sales Return
Journal to the Sales Return Account — Option (iv).
