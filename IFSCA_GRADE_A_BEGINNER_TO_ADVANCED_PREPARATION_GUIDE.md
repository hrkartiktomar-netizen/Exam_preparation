# IFSCA Grade A General Stream: Complete Beginner To Final Selection Guide

For complete beginners who are starting with Class 6-10 NCERT maths and need a disciplined path to IFSCA Grade A General Stream readiness.

---

## Start Here

You do not need to know IFSCA on day one. You need to build the ability to understand finance, regulators, data, current affairs and exam questions without panic. The preparation has to move in this order:

1. Build arithmetic, reading and basic economy/accountancy foundations.
2. Convert that base into Phase I speed through quant, reasoning, English and financial awareness practice.
3. Learn Phase I Paper 2 subjects: economy, commerce, accountancy, management, finance and costing.
4. Learn IFSCA from the Act and ecosystem, not from random coaching summaries.
5. Learn Phase II domains: IFSCA/GIFT, Budget/Economic Survey, banking, capital markets, insurance and pension.
6. Solve PYQs and mocks only after you can explain the concepts behind the answers.
7. Keep descriptive English running in parallel because Phase II Paper 1 can eliminate you.

The mistake to avoid is starting with IFSCA regulations immediately. If you do that without maths, economy, finance and regulator vocabulary, you will only memorise fragments. Start simple, but do not stay simple.

---

## What You Are Preparing For

The General Stream exam is not a normal banking exam and not a pure commerce exam. It is a regulator exam built around:

- speed and accuracy in Phase I,
- breadth in Phase I Paper 2,
- serious financial-sector understanding in Phase II,
- current official IFSCA ecosystem knowledge,
- ability to write clearly in English,
- disciplined negative-marking control.

### Phase I

| Paper | Content | Marks | Duration | Minimum cutoff |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Paper 1 | General awareness financial sector, English, quantitative aptitude, reasoning | 100 | 60 min | 30% |
| Paper 2 | General Stream domain paper | 100 | 60 min | 40% |
| Aggregate | Paper 1 + Paper 2 | 200 | 120 min | 40% aggregate |

Phase I is only for shortlisting to Phase II. Do not ignore it, but do not build your whole preparation around it.

Paper 1 has:

- General awareness, financial sector: 25 questions.
- English language: 25 questions.
- Quantitative aptitude: 25 questions.
- Reasoning: 25 questions.

Paper 2 covers:

- general knowledge and current events,
- economic and social development,
- commerce and accountancy,
- management,
- finance,
- costing,
- Indian economy and global economy,
- central government initiatives/schemes in the financial sector.

### Phase II

| Paper | Content | Marks | Duration | Minimum cutoff | Weight inside Phase II |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Paper 1 | English descriptive: precis, essay, comprehension | 100 | 60 min | 30% | 1/3 |
| Paper 2 | General Stream domain paper | 100 | 60 min | 40% | 2/3 |
| Aggregate | Weighted score | 100 | 120 min | 40% aggregate | 100% |

Phase II Paper 2 is the main exam. Paper 1 matters, but Paper 2 is the gatekeeper and has 2/3 weight.

Phase II Paper 2 covers:

- IFSCA Act, IFSCA, IFSC, GIFT IFSC, GIFT City and global financial centres,
- Union Budget and Economic Survey,
- banking,
- capital markets,
- insurance,
- pension sector.

Final selection uses Phase II and interview in an 85:15 ratio. Interview matters, but it cannot compensate for weak Phase II marks.

### Negative Marking

There is 1/4 negative marking in objective papers:

- Phase I Paper 1,
- Phase I Paper 2,
- Phase II Paper 2.

This means your preparation must build judgement, not just attempt speed. A candidate who attempts less but knows what to leave can beat a candidate who guesses aggressively.

---

## Mentor Verdict On The Existing Preparation Problem

If you are starting from Class 6 maths, the correct strategy is not to rush. But it also cannot be a slow school-style journey forever. You need staged conversion:

- NCERT gives language and conceptual comfort.
- R.S. Aggarwal type books build exam-level arithmetic.
- Sectional tests build speed.
- Official syllabus tells you what not to study.
- PYQs tell you the depth and style.
- Annual reports and bulletins give current IFSCA facts.
- ICSI material explains regulation in an organised way.

Your preparation should feel like climbing stairs:

1. Can I understand the sentence?
2. Can I solve the basic problem?
3. Can I solve it under time pressure?
4. Can I connect it to IFSCA/regulator context?
5. Can I revise it quickly before the exam?

If a resource does not help one of these steps, do not let it consume time.

## What The PYQs And Handouts Suggest

The official handouts and available PYQ/memory-paper set point to a clear pattern.

### Phase I

Phase I is not just aptitude. Paper 1 is speed. Paper 2 is a broad domain screen.

The official handout samples show:

- English: grammar, vocabulary, sentence correction, comprehension.
- Quant: computation, quantitative reasoning, table/graph interpretation, approximation and arithmetic.
- Reasoning: word rearrangement, syllogism/inference, logical thinking, puzzles.
- General awareness: finance-sector awareness mixed with broader current/static awareness.

The Phase I memory/PYQ material repeatedly points toward:

- commerce/accountancy/management/finance/costing basics,
- Indian economy and macroeconomy,
- banking and financial institutions,
- capital markets and instruments,
- Budget/Economic Survey/current events,
- some insurance/pension/IFSCA awareness.

Conclusion for Phase I:

- Do not prepare Paper 1 like a high-level CAT exam.
- Do not prepare Paper 2 like only IFSCA.
- Paper 2 needs broad financial literacy and commerce basics before IFSCA depth.

### Phase II

The Phase II memory/PYQ material is much more concentrated around:

- insurance,
- banking,
- pension,
- capital markets,
- IFSCA/GIFT/IFSC core,
- Budget/Economic Survey,
- bullion and newer IFSC niche sectors.

Conclusion for Phase II:

- Banking, capital markets, insurance and pension are not optional side topics. They are core.
- IFSCA Act and GIFT IFSC must be understood as the framework connecting all sectors.
- Budget/Economic Survey is not just current affairs; it gives macro policy language for MCQs and essays.
- Bullion, fintech, payments, aircraft/ship leasing and sustainable finance are not first-month topics, but they matter after your core is built.

## The Learning Ladder

This is the order you should follow from absolute beginner level.

### Ladder 1: School Foundation

Goal: stop being afraid of numbers, English paragraphs and economy terms.

Study:

- Class 6-10 NCERT Mathematics.
- Class 9-10 NCERT Economics.
- Class 8-10 English reading.

Output:

- arithmetic notebook,
- formula notebook,
- vocabulary notebook,
- basic economy definitions notebook.

Move on when:

- you can solve percentage, ratio, average, profit/loss and simple interest without looking at solutions,
- you can read a business-news article and summarise it in 5 lines,
- you can explain GDP, inflation, bank, tax, budget and poverty in simple language.

### Ladder 2: Class 11-12 Foundation

Goal: understand the language of Phase I Paper 2.

Study:

- Class 11 Indian Economic Development.
- Class 12 Macroeconomics.
- Class 11 Statistics for Economics.
- Class 11 Accountancy basics.
- Class 12 Accountancy selected chapters.
- Class 11-12 Business Studies for management.

Output:

- 100 economy definitions,
- 50 accountancy terms,
- 20 management short notes,
- 30 basic data interpretation sets.

Move on when:

- you can explain fiscal deficit, monetary policy, balance of payments, national income and financial markets,
- you can read a balance sheet/cash-flow style question at a basic level,
- you can solve simple statistics and DI questions.

### Ladder 3: Competitive Exam Conversion

Goal: convert knowledge into Phase I marks.

Books:

- R.S. Aggarwal Quantitative Aptitude.
- R.S. Aggarwal Verbal and Non-Verbal Reasoning.
- S.P. Bakshi or Wren and Martin for English.

Practice:

- 40 quant questions per day.
- 30 reasoning questions per day.
- 25 English questions per day.
- 20 financial awareness notes per week.

Move on when:

- Paper 1 sectional mocks are consistently above 60/100,
- quant accuracy is above 80% in untimed practice,
- reasoning accuracy is above 80% outside hard puzzles,
- English errors are tracked and declining.

### Ladder 4: Phase I Paper 2 Domain

Goal: become financially literate enough for broad objective questions.

Study blocks:

- economy and social development,
- commerce and accountancy,
- management,
- finance,
- costing,
- Indian/global economy,
- government financial-sector schemes.

Sources:

- NCERTs first,
- standard economy/accountancy references next,
- official Budget/Economic Survey summaries,
- regulator websites for financial-sector schemes and institutions,
- PYQs after first reading.

Move on when:

- you can attempt mixed Paper 2 tests above 55/100,
- you can identify why an answer is correct, not just remember it,
- you have one-page notes for each syllabus block.

### Ladder 5: IFSCA Core

Goal: understand what IFSCA is and why GIFT IFSC exists.

Study in this exact order:

1. IFSCA Act basics.
2. IFSC and GIFT IFSC concept.
3. IFSCA as unified regulator.
4. Financial products, financial services, financial institutions.
5. Role of RBI, SEBI, IRDAI and PFRDA before/alongside IFSCA.
6. Annual report highlights.
7. Bulletins for current ecosystem numbers.
8. ICSI Paper 4.6 lessons.

Move on when you can explain:

- why India created IFSCA,
- what GIFT IFSC does,
- why foreign currency and cross-border finance matter,
- how banking, capital market, insurance, pension and funds fit under IFSCA,
- what makes IFSCA different from a normal domestic regulator.

### Ladder 6: Phase II Specialisation

Goal: score in the actual final-selection paper.

Build six notebooks:

1. IFSCA/GIFT/global financial centres.
2. Budget/Economic Survey.
3. Banking.
4. Capital markets and bullion.
5. Insurance.
6. Pension sector.

Every notebook must have:

- definitions,
- key institutions,
- current data,
- official source references,
- 100 MCQs,
- 20 short notes,
- 5 essay/interview points.

Move on when:

- Phase II Paper 2 mocks are consistently above 65/100,
- you can write one-page explanations without copying,
- you can revise each notebook in under 2 hours.

## Math Roadmap From Class 6 To Exam Level

You are starting correctly with Class 6-10 NCERT. Use it as foundation, not as the final quant source.

### Class 6-8 NCERT

Focus on:

- whole numbers,
- fractions,
- decimals,
- ratio and proportion,
- percentages,
- basic algebra,
- simple equations,
- mensuration,
- data handling.

Skip or do lightly:

- construction-heavy geometry,
- proof-heavy parts,
- decorative activities that do not build arithmetic.

Target:

- 70%+ accuracy in basic arithmetic without calculator.

### Class 9-10 NCERT

Focus on:

- number systems basics,
- linear equations,
- quadratic equations basics,
- arithmetic progression,
- coordinate geometry basics,
- statistics,
- probability,
- trigonometry only at basic formula level,
- mensuration.

Do not overinvest in:

- long geometry proofs,
- advanced trigonometric transformations,
- board-style derivations.

Target:

- solve percentages, averages, ratio, SI/CI and DI without hesitation.

### After NCERT: R.S. Aggarwal Quantitative Aptitude

Study order:

1. Simplification and approximation.
2. Number system.
3. Percentage.
4. Ratio and proportion.
5. Average.
6. Profit and loss.
7. Simple and compound interest.
8. Time and work.
9. Time, speed and distance.
10. Mixture/alligation.
11. Permutation and combination basics.
12. Probability basics.
13. Data interpretation.

Do not move to shortcuts before basics. Shortcuts only help when the concept is stable.

### Quant Readiness Benchmarks

| Stage | Benchmark |
|---|---|
| NCERT basic | 50 arithmetic questions in 90 minutes with 80% accuracy |
| R.S. Aggarwal early | 40 mixed questions in 75 minutes with 75% accuracy |
| Sectional exam level | 25 questions in 20-22 minutes with 80% accuracy |
| Final Phase I level | no blind guessing, DI tables readable under time pressure |

## English Roadmap

English has two jobs:

- Phase I objective marks,
- Phase II descriptive survival and scoring.

### Foundation

Daily:

- read one editorial/business article,
- write a 100-word summary,
- note 10 useful words,
- revise grammar rules.

Study:

- subject-verb agreement,
- tenses,
- articles,
- prepositions,
- modifiers,
- sentence correction,
- para jumbles,
- reading comprehension.

### Descriptive English

Start early. Do not wait for Phase I result.

Practice every week:

- 1 precis,
- 1 essay,
- 1 comprehension set.

Common essay themes:

- GIFT IFSC as India's international financial gateway,
- financial inclusion and digital finance,
- sustainable finance,
- global financial centres,
- fintech opportunities and risks,
- insurance penetration,
- pension coverage,
- role of regulators,
- Budget and growth,
- capital markets and infrastructure finance.

Good essay structure:

1. Define the issue.
2. Give context.
3. Explain opportunities.
4. Explain risks.
5. Explain regulator/governance angle.
6. Conclude with balanced reform path.

Readiness:

- 600-word essay in 35 minutes,
- precis in 20 minutes,
- comprehension answers precise and not over-written.

## IFSCA Core Roadmap

Do not read IFSCA like a list of circulars. Read it like a story:

1. India needed an international financial services centre.
2. GIFT City provides the physical and legal location.
3. IFSC units deal with international financial services, often in foreign currency.
4. Multiple domestic regulators would create overlap.
5. IFSCA became the unified regulator.
6. IFSCA now regulates/develops banking, capital markets, insurance, funds, bullion, fintech, payments, leasing and other financial services in IFSC.

### IFSCA Act

Know:

- definition of Authority,
- financial product,
- financial service,
- financial institution,
- International Financial Services Centre,
- composition of Authority,
- powers and functions,
- power to issue directions,
- power to make regulations,
- overriding effect,
- relationship with other regulators.

Make a 20-page Act notebook. Do not copy the Act; translate it into exam language.

### GIFT IFSC Ecosystem

Know:

- GIFT City,
- GIFT IFSC,
- SEZ vs Domestic Tariff Area,
- IFSC Banking Units,
- IFSC exchanges,
- Fund Management Entities,
- IFSC Insurance Offices,
- IFSC Insurance Intermediary Offices,
- India International Bullion Exchange,
- FinTech sandbox,
- payment services,
- aircraft and ship leasing,
- sustainable finance and ESG debt.

### Current Data

Maintain a one-page data sheet from IFSCA annual reports and bulletins:

- number of registered/authorised entities,
- banking assets,
- IBU count,
- exchange turnover,
- debt listings,
- FME/fund count,
- commitments raised,
- insurance premium data,
- bullion exchange participants,
- aircraft/ship leasing data,
- sustainable finance and ESG debt numbers.

Update it monthly from official sources.

## The 12-Month Plan

Use this if you are starting almost from zero.

### Months 1-2: Repair The Base

Daily:

- 90 min Class 6-8 maths.
- 45 min English reading and grammar.
- 45 min Class 9-10 economics.
- 30 min vocabulary/current affairs.
- 30 min revision.

Weekly:

- 150 arithmetic problems,
- 5 English summaries,
- 30 economy definitions,
- 1 small test.

Target:

- arithmetic no longer feels scary,
- editorials are understandable,
- economy words start making sense.

### Months 3-4: Finish School Foundation And Start Aptitude

Daily:

- 75 min Class 9-10 maths/R.S. Aggarwal basics,
- 60 min reasoning,
- 45 min English objective,
- 45 min economy/accountancy basics,
- 30 min financial current affairs.

Weekly:

- 250 quant questions,
- 200 reasoning questions,
- 150 English questions,
- 1 sectional mock,
- 1 economy/accountancy revision sheet.

Target:

- Paper 1 mock around 50-60/100 and improving.

### Months 5-6: Phase I Paper 2 Foundation

Daily:

- 60 min economy,
- 60 min commerce/accountancy,
- 45 min management/finance/costing,
- 45 min objective MCQs,
- 45 min Paper 1 maintenance.

Weekly:

- 300 domain MCQs,
- 1 Phase I full mock,
- 1 Budget/Economic Survey update,
- 1 revision sheet.

Target:

- Phase I Paper 2 mock above 50/100.

### Months 7-8: IFSCA Core

Daily:

- 75 min IFSCA Act/GIFT/IFSC,
- 60 min annual report/bulletin data,
- 60 min ICSI structured chapter,
- 45 min MCQs,
- 30 min descriptive English.

Weekly:

- 2 IFSCA topic notes,
- 1 data sheet update,
- 200 IFSCA/domain MCQs,
- 2 descriptive answers.

Target:

- you can explain IFSCA in 3 minutes without notes.

### Months 9-10: Phase II Specialisation

Weekly rotation:

- Monday: IFSCA/GIFT/global financial centres.
- Tuesday: Budget/Economic Survey.
- Wednesday: banking.
- Thursday: capital markets and bullion.
- Friday: insurance.
- Saturday: pension and mixed revision.
- Sunday: mock and analysis.

Daily:

- 90 min domain study,
- 60 min MCQs,
- 45 min descriptive English,
- 30 min revision/data sheet.

Target:

- Phase II Paper 2 mock above 60/100.

### Month 11: PYQ And Mock Consolidation

Tasks:

- solve all available IFSCA memory/PYQ papers,
- tag every question by syllabus block,
- create an error register,
- revise weak topics from official/ICSI sources,
- take two Phase I mocks and two Phase II mocks per week.

Error labels:

- concept not known,
- fact not remembered,
- calculation error,
- reading error,
- time pressure,
- careless guess.

Target:

- Phase I 130+/200 in mocks,
- Phase II Paper 2 65+/100 in mocks,
- descriptive English 60+/100.

### Month 12: Final Selection Mode

Do:

- revise notification and pattern,
- revise all six Phase II notebooks,
- revise IFSCA data sheet,
- revise Budget/Economic Survey,
- revise mistakes,
- reattempt wrong PYQs,
- practise under negative marking.

Do not:

- start bulky new books,
- collect new random PDFs,
- switch strategies,
- ignore sleep and typing practice.

## Mock Strategy

### Phase I

Sequence:

1. Sectional untimed practice.
2. Sectional timed tests.
3. Paper 1 mocks.
4. Paper 2 mocks.
5. Full Phase I mocks.

Attempt strategy:

- Paper 1: maximise accurate attempts.
- Paper 2: avoid guesses in unfamiliar domain questions.
- Leave questions if you cannot eliminate at least two options.

### Phase II

Sequence:

1. Topic MCQs.
2. Mixed domain MCQs.
3. Phase II Paper 2 mocks.
4. Descriptive English tests.
5. Full Phase II simulation.

Analysis:

- For every wrong answer, write source of correction.
- For every guessed correct answer, mark it as weak.
- For every missed question, decide whether it was a knowledge gap or time decision.

## Interview Preparation

Interview has 15% weight, but only Phase II qualifiers reach it. Start after Phase II basics are stable.

Prepare:

- your background,
- why IFSCA,
- why Grade A,
- what IFSCA does,
- GIFT IFSC current ecosystem,
- latest Budget and Economic Survey,
- banking/capital market/insurance/pension basics,
- one deep area of personal strength,
- recent IFSCA updates.

Interview answers must be:

- factual,
- concise,
- balanced,
- regulator-minded,
- not coaching-scripted.

Practise:

- 50 personal questions,
- 100 IFSCA factual questions,
- 50 opinion/current affairs questions.

## Readiness Checklist

You are not ready just because you have read many PDFs. You are ready when:

- you can reproduce the exam pattern and cutoffs from memory,
- your Phase I mocks are consistently above 130/200,
- your Phase II Paper 2 mocks are consistently above 65/100,
- you have written at least 25 descriptive English answers,
- you have revised IFSCA Act basics at least three times,
- you have a current IFSCA data sheet,
- you have read annual report highlights and latest bulletins,
- you have Budget/Economic Survey notes,
- you have tagged PYQs by syllabus block,
- your error log is shrinking,
- you can explain GIFT IFSC to a non-finance person in 3 minutes,
- you can explain why IFSCA matters to India's financial system.

## What Not To Do

Do not:

- start with advanced regulations before NCERT/economy basics,
- treat coaching PDFs as authority,
- ignore Phase II until Phase I result,
- skip descriptive English,
- solve mocks without analysis,
- collect PDFs instead of revising,
- memorise data without understanding,
- study current affairs without regulator linkage,
- guess aggressively despite negative marking,
- change books every week.

Do:

- build from NCERT,
- move to exam books,
- use official sources for truth,
- use ICSI for structure,
- use PYQs for practice,
- revise repeatedly,
- maintain error logs,
- keep one clean data sheet,
- practise writing.

## Final Mentor Note

The exam is beatable, but only if you respect its shape. It is not enough to be good at maths. It is not enough to read IFSCA circulars. It is not enough to buy mocks. You need a layered preparation:

- foundation,
- speed,
- financial literacy,
- IFSCA core,
- Phase II depth,
- current affairs,
- descriptive writing,
- mock analysis.

Your first job is not to look advanced. Your first job is to become consistent. If you start from Class 6 maths and keep climbing the ladder without skipping stages, you can reach the level needed for this exam.
